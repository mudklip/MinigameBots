=== start | Getting started
# What this is
An AI that plays Minecraft as you, with a real (virtual) keyboard and mouse. You teach it by playing, by letting teachers play, or by letting it practice.

> New: [F10] > [★ Training Hub] trains your AI for you. Start there.

# The 3 keys to remember
- [F10] opens this menu
- [F8] gives the AI control, or takes it back
- [F9] emergency stop: the AI lets go of everything at once

> You never need to be good at Minecraft: Teacher demos and practice can train it without you.
> Press [F1] on any MinigameBots screen to jump to its page here.

=== hub | Training Hub (start here)
The easiest way to make your AI better: pick a goal and a time, the Coach does the rest.

1. [F10] > [★ Training Hub]
2. Click a goal: [PvP], [Aim], [Movement], [Mining] or [Survival]
3. Pick a time: [30 min], [2 hours] or [Overnight]
4. Press [Start coaching] and let it run

# What the Coach does (PvP example)
- Builds a practice arena where you stand (stand somewhere open!)
- Starts a duel against the godly teacher and records Fighter demos
- Trains on them, then practices (in 6 worlds at once for Lite AIs)
- Takes an exam against the league, then repeats practice + exam until time is up

# Levels, ratings and achievements
- [Lv] grows with effort (training, practice, exams)
- PvP [win%] and Aim [score] only move when the AI really got better
- Achievements mark milestones: hover the badges at the bottom

# Watch & learn
- [Watch & learn: on] (top of the Hub): while you play or spectate, other players' fights are recorded and turned into demos every 10 minutes
- Good moments count more, the ones that got them killed count nothing
- The PvP plan trains on them automatically
! Only use Watch & learn where you're allowed to record other players.
> Want full control? [Advanced...] opens all the detailed screens.

=== keys | Keys and controls
# Hotkeys
- [F7] start / stop recording yourself
- [F8] AI takes control / you take it back
- [F9] emergency stop
- [F10] MinigameBots menu
- [F1] (in a MinigameBots screen) help for that screen

# Extra keys (not set by default)
Set them in Options > Controls > MinigameBots:
- [Mark good] this moment was good: learn it more
- [Mark mistake] this moment was bad: never copy it
- [Mark base] remember this spot as your base

> Marks work while recording. A few "mistake" marks are worth more than a lot of extra footage.

=== brain | The brain (setup)
The brain is the AI's thinking part. It runs on your PC in the background; nothing is sent online.

# Set it up once
1. [F10] > [Brain...]
2. Press [Install (GPU)] (or [Install (CPU)] without an NVIDIA card)
3. Wait for the download (a few GB, one time)

# Foundations (optional)
- [Brain...] > [Foundations] installs OpenAI's VPT models: they already know basic Minecraft from watching 70,000 hours of video
- With one installed, choose [Base: VPT] when training for a smarter start

! VPT needs a GPU and learns from the picture only. [Lite] works on any PC.

=== profiles | AI profiles
Each AI is a profile with its own name, skin, memories and training.

# Make one
1. [F10] > [AI Profiles...]
2. Click [New AI], give it a name (and a skin: any player name)
3. Select it and press [Use this AI]

# Buttons
- [Edit...] name, skin, and its human limits (reaction time, turn speed)
- [Duplicate] copy an AI to experiment safely
- [Merge] make a new AI that takes each skill from one of two AIs
- [Export] / [Import] share an AI as a file (exports folder)

> Duplicate before big experiments: the original stays untouched.

=== recording | Recording yourself
Recordings are the easiest way to teach: it copies what you do.

1. Press [F7] and play normally
2. Press [F7] again to stop (it saves by itself)
3. Open [F10] > [Recordings...] to watch, trim, rename and tag them

# Make recordings count
- Record the thing you want it to learn, a few minutes at a time
- Use [Mark mistake] on bad moments; they are skipped
- Variety helps: different places, times of day, situations

> Recordings also teach other players' moves (see "Learning from players").

=== training | Training
# Train an AI
1. [AI Profiles...] > select the AI > [Train]
2. Tick the recordings to learn from (left list)
3. Pick options (right), press [Start]
4. Watch the progress; press [Stop] any time (it keeps what it learned)

# Options
- [Base]: [Lite] (fast, any PC) or [VPT] (smarter start, GPU)
- [PC]: Quiet / Balanced / Max: how hard your PC works
- [Plan]: how long. Quick try to test, Overnight for real results
- [Skill]: train one skill ("combat", "mining"...) instead of the main brain
- [Start fresh]: forget everything and start over
- [Aim drill]: also learn aiming in a tiny built-in simulator (very fast, Lite only)

# Buttons on top
- [Realism...] make it play more like a human (see "Realism")
- [Versions...] go back to an earlier version if training made it worse
- [Practice...] / [Exams...] learn by trying, and test it

> A version is saved automatically before every training. Nothing is ever lost.

=== teachers | Teacher demos
Teachers are scripted experts that play AS your AI while it records. Free expert demos, no skill needed.

1. [Train] screen > [Teacher demos]
2. Pick a teacher and a length, press [Start recording]
3. Stop any time with [F9]; the recording appears in the Train list

# Teachers
- [Fighter] crits, W-taps, shield timing, axe on shields, strafing (see "Get really good at PvP")
- [Aim and chase] steady aim at the nearest target
- [Baritone] travel, mine ores, go home (only if the optional Baritone jar is in mods)

> Start a Training Grounds match first (e.g. against a "godly" dummy) so every round is recorded.

=== commands | Talk to your AI (/ai)
Tell your AI what to do in plain words. Only you can: it's a command on your own PC, it never reaches the server.

# Examples
- [/ai mine 10 iron then go home]
- [/ai hunt 3 cows], [/ai kill a zombie]
- [/ai follow me], [/ai protect Alex], [/ai come back]
- [/ai explore for 5 minutes], [/ai do whatever you want]
- [/ai stop]

# What happens
1. It turns your words into goals (the same ones as the Goals screen)
2. It answers in your chat, e.g. "Okay: mine iron x10, then go home"
3. Your selected AI takes control and does them in order

# Free-form commands (optional)
- Common phrasings work straight away
- For anything else, install the mind: [F10] > [Brain...] > [Mind (3 GB)]
- It runs on your PC only, and can only choose goals: never anything you couldn't do yourself

> Chain with "then", "and" or commas: [/ai get food, then go home, then explore].

=== chat | Chat in character
Give your AI a personality: it chats when people talk to it, while it plays.

# Set it up
1. Install the mind once: [F10] > [Brain...] > [Mind (3 GB)] (or [Lite mind (1 GB)])
2. [AI Profiles] > select the AI > [Edit...] > [Character...]
3. Write the character, or press [Make one for me] and edit what it invents
4. Turn [Chat: on], press [Done], then [Done] again to save

# When does it talk?
- Only while it's playing ([F8])
- Only when someone says its character name or your name
- At most one short line every 8 seconds, tagged [AI] (Settings)

# Character fields
- [Name in chat] what it calls itself
- [Personality], [How it talks], [About it]
- [Never talk about] topics it will skip (comma-separated)

> It all runs on your PC. The same mind also understands free-form [/ai] commands.
! A small model: fun for banter, not deep conversation. It can be repetitive or odd now and then.

=== voice | Talk with your voice
Hold a key, say what you want, let go. It works like typing [/ai].

# Set it up (once)
1. [F10] > [Brain...] > [Voice (350 MB)] (about 350 MB, runs on your PC only)
2. Optional: [Settings] > Autonomy and safety > [Speak replies aloud]

# Use it
1. Hold [B] (change it in Options > Controls > MinigameBots)
2. Say e.g. "mine ten iron then come home"
3. Let go: it writes what it heard and what it'll do in your chat

> Free-form sentences need the mind too ([Mind (3 GB)] in Brain); simple ones work without it.
! Only your own microphone is used: nobody else can talk to your AI this way.

=== core | The Core (common sense)
The Core is the first card in the Skills list (the light block). It's common sense shared by every skill: in a new minigame it works out the rules by itself.

# Learn a minigame
1. Join the minigame (any server or map).
2. Training Hub > Skills > [Core] > [Learn this game].
3. It plays and watches what happens: what knocks players out, what scores, what wins. After a few rounds the rules appear on the Core screen, for example "my hit with snowball -> I get a kill (90% sure)" and "getting hit by snowball -> I'm out".
4. It rewards itself by those rules, borrows skills that fit (snowballs use its archery aim, melee uses its sword skills) and keeps learning the game's details.
> Games are remembered by their scoreboard title: next time it starts out knowing the rules.
! It ignores coincidences: a rule needs to happen a few times, clearly more often than by chance.

=== skills | Skills (one by one)
Every skill your AI can learn has its own card: [F10] > [Skills], or [Skills...] in the Training Hub. The tabs along the top sort them by kind: Combat, Movement, Mining, Building, Farming, Survival, Inventory and your own.

# Practice a skill
1. Click its card.
2. Pick how long (e.g. [30 min]) and press [Practice].
3. It sets up what it needs (arena, dummy, duel or aim trainer) and practices by itself. Open the Training Hub to watch progress, [F9] to stop.
! Skill practice needs a Lite-based AI, and Training Grounds for anything that practices in an arena.

# Practice areas (no setup)
Most skills build their own practice area around you when you press [Practice], and remove it again (the ground put back exactly) when the plan ends: a mining block with ores, a tree grove, a wheat field, an animal pen, a monster pen, a bridge gap, a parkour course, a build pad, a pool, a messy inventory. Tools it needs are handed over. Command: /tg area <kind>, /tg area clear.

# Change what it learns
- [Rewarded for]: what counts. [Edit rewards] lets you switch each one on or off, make it stronger [+] or weaker [-], flip a reward into a penalty (click the number), add new ones or remove them.
- [Not allowed while practicing]: click a pill to lock that action, e.g. lock [Walking] and [Inventory & menus] for aim training so it only aims. Locked actions are never pressed, whatever the AI wants.
- [Stages]: each skill learns in steps. [Basics] gives strong hints and learns fast (it copies anything that works), [Mastery] gives no hints and only polishes. Change each stage's speed, how many tries before it moves on, and its [Hints].
- Hints are extra rewards for the early stages, like "keep holding break until the block breaks". They switch off by themselves in later stages.
> ✦ marks a self-discovery stage: it tries its own ideas, e.g. working out which of several swords to use when.

# Never do (for everything)
Training Hub > [Never do...]: actions this AI never uses, whatever it's doing: free play, goals, plans, practice, training worlds. A skill's own list can block more, never less.

# Your own skills
1. Press the dashed [+ Add a skill] card at the end of any list.
2. Start [From scratch], or copy any ready-made skill and change it.
3. Click the icon to pick any item as its icon, give it a name, then add rewards.
- Ready-made skills can be edited too. [Reset] puts one back as shipped (what it learned is kept).

# Advanced
[Advanced...] has the finer settings: its own network or the shared one, learning speed, exploration, how close to stay to its demo behaviour, a teacher for its first lessons, a target creature, adding or removing stages. [Every detail...] on the rewards screen opens the full rule editor (conditions, caps, cooldowns, forgiving).

=== backups | Backups
A backup is a copy of the whole AI: every network (what it learned), skill settings and stages, levels and exams, memory, your objectives, and its name, skin and character. Open [Backups...] in the Training Hub or the Skills screen.

# Make one
- Press [+ Back up now] whenever you like (say, before trying something risky).
- One is also made by itself before coaching or practicing, at most every few hours. The last 5 automatic ones are kept; press [Keep] on any backup to keep it forever.

# Go back
1. Click a backup on the left.
2. To go back completely, press [Bring back everything].
3. To bring back only some parts, untick the rest, then press [Bring back ticked parts]. Each skill is its own part: [Skill: Sword PvP] is its settings and stage, [Learned: ...] is what it learned. For example, tick only those to bring back an old PvP and keep everything else.
! Skills in one area share what they learn by default (e.g. all combat skills learn into [Learned: combat (shared)]), so they go back together. To restore one skill alone, give it its own network first: its card > [Advanced...] > [Network: its own].
! Going back to an older version replaces what it learned since then. You'll be asked first, with the option to [Back up first, then go back], so you can always return to how it is now. It never stops you.
> The network history in [Advanced] > [Versions] works the same way for a single network, and warns the same way.

=== goals | Goals
The Manager decides what to do moment to moment, using the AI's skills.

# Give it goals
1. [F10] > [Goals...]
2. Pick a goal type (hunt, mine, explore, go to, follow, protect...), fill in details, [Add]
3. Press [Start]: it works through the list in order

# Free play
- With [Free play: on], after the list it does what it likes
- Sliders set what it likes: exploring, mining, hunting
- It always goes home at night (if it has a base) and finds food when hungry

# It interrupts a goal to
- fight back when attacked, or run away when it would lose
- eat when hungry

> An untrained AI can already do simple goals (like hunting a pig) with built-in reflexes.

# Attitude
[Attitude] (bottom of the Goals screen) sets what it's about when it plays by itself:
- [Neutral]: lives like a normal player (farms, mines, builds, stocks up). Fights monsters and anyone who attacks it for real.
- [Hunt players]: gears up first (mines iron, then diamonds), then looks for players and fights the ones it can beat.
- [Guard]: stays near its base or the player it protects; fights anything hostile nearby.
- [Peaceful]: never fights players; runs from real attacks.
- [From personality]: picked once from its character's personality (by the mind if installed, else from key words), and again only if the personality changes.
Needs compete all the time: fear, anger, hunger, duty and boredom. The Goals screen shows what it feels while it plays.

# Joke hits or real attacks?
A single light punch from a friend is a joke and is ignored. A weapon, real damage, several hits in a row, crits, or an enemy turns it into a real attack, and it reacts (fights back, or runs if it's peaceful or losing).

# Counting health
Like a sharp player, it doesn't read other players' health from the game: it counts it. It remembers its own hits (weapon, charge, crits, their visible armour, Protection IV assumed on enchanted pieces), hits from others, totems popped and golden apples eaten. The longer since a hit, the less sure it is, because they may have healed. Settings > Perception > [Count others' health] (on by default).

=== practice | Practice and exams
Practice = it tries things and improves from rewards, on its own.

# Practice
1. [Train] screen > [Practice...]
2. Pick what to practice: Duel, Hunt, Survive, Aim trainer, Crit / W-tap / Shield-break drills, [Figure It Out] (any minigame)
3. Press [Start practicing] and leave it running

# Exams
- [Exams...] scores the AI over a few match rounds without learning
- Compare results over time to see if training helps

> Practice is best inside a Training Grounds match: rounds reset by themselves.
! Practice works with the Lite base.

=== grounds | Training Grounds
The second mod (minigamebots-training). It gives you and the AI things to train against: dummies that fight back, an aim trainer, arenas that reset instantly, and matches.

# Open it
1. Join a world (singleplayer works, no cheats needed)
2. Type [/tg] in chat and press Enter
3. A menu opens: click [Arenas], [Matches], [Training dummies] or [Aim trainer]

> Everything in the menu also has a command: type [/tg ] and press [Tab] to see them.
! On a server, it must be installed on the server too, and only operators can manage it.

=== pvp | Get really good at PvP
The route to a strong fighter, with no PvP skill needed from you. Each step builds on the last.

# Step 1: copy an expert
1. Make an arena and a duel: [/tg minigame duel duel1 m1]
2. Make the opponent the cheating teacher: [/tg match opponent m1 blue godly]
3. [/tg match join m1 red], [/tg match start m1]
4. [Train] > [Teacher demos] > [Fighter (mixed)], 10-30 minutes > [Start recording]
5. Optional: tick [Aim drill] for extra aiming practice

# Step 2: train on it
1. [Train]: tick the "Teacher: Fighter" recordings, [Plan: Overnight], [Start]
2. It now fights like the teacher: crits, W-taps, shield timing

# Step 3: get better than the teacher
1. Set the opponent to the league: [/tg match opponent m1 blue league]
2. [Practice...] > [Duel] > [Start practicing], leave it running
3. Later, switch to self-play: [/tg match opponent m1 blue self]

# Step 4: check progress
- [Exams...] scores it over a few rounds; compare over time
- Worse after a session? [Versions...] > [Go back]

> The league keeps picking the opponent styles it loses to, so practice always targets its weak spots.
! Baritone is not part of this: it's a walking/mining bot. The fighting teacher is built in.

=== multiworld | Multi-world training
# Spectate a training world
1. Training Hub > [⚡ Worlds] tab (or [F10] while worlds are running).
2. Click any world's card: your camera follows that world's AI up close, from its own eyes, even across new rounds.
3. Press [F10] again to see all worlds, click another world to switch, or [Back to my view] to return where you were (your game mode is restored).
> Command: /tg multitrain watch <number>, and /tg multitrain watch off.

The easy way: Training Hub > [⚡ Worlds]. Pick how many worlds and the opponent, press [Start]. The worlds are built in a compact block around you (4 = 2x2, 8 = a ring around you) and removed when you stop, leaving the ground as it was. The screen then shows every world live: a mini-map (green = your AI facing up, red = its opponent), both health bars and the score. Click a world to follow its learner up close; [Back to my view] returns you. Cards flash green on a win, red on a loss.

Practice in many arena copies at once: 8 worlds = about 8x the practice per second.

# Start
1. Use an AI on the [Lite] base (pictures aren't drawn for the extra worlds)
2. Save an arena (see "Arenas and matches")
3. [/tg multitrain start duel1 8 L3] (arena, how many copies, opponent style)
4. Your AI takes control by itself and trains in all copies; you just watch
5. [/tg multitrain stop] when done (progress is saved as it goes)

# Tips
- Start with 4-8 copies; more needs a faster PC
- [/tick sprint 6000] runs the game as fast as your PC allows for a while
- Use a harder opponent ([L5], [godly]) once it starts winning
- Check progress with [Exams...]; a version is saved before practice

> Copies are built next to the arena, in the direction of +X: keep that space empty.
! Lite base only. Your own player stands still while it trains.

=== dummies | Fight a dummy
Dummies are fake players that stand, move, block and fight back. Use them to practice yourself, or to record and train the AI.

# Spawn one
1. Type [/tg dummy spawn godly] (it appears where you stand)
2. Hit it! Its health and your DPS show above its head

# Styles (the word after spawn)
- [L1] punching bag (never moves) ... [L5] sweaty
- [rusher] runs at you and spam-clicks
- [blocker] holds a shield up between hits
- [critter] jump-crits, [w_tapper] sprint-resets for knockback
- [kiter] backs off, [godly] perfect teacher (it cheats!)

# Change or remove
- Sneak + right-click a dummy: its settings menu (style, shield, armour, health...)
- [/tg dummy remove] removes dummies near you

> Want your AI to fight it? Spawn one that fights back ([L3] and up, or [godly]) and press [F8]: the AI fights whatever attacks it. [F8] again to take back control.

=== aim | Aim trainer
Targets appear around you; hit them as fast as you can. Scores and personal bests are kept.

# Start
- [/tg aim flick] targets pop up, hit each one quickly
- [/tg aim track] keep your crosshair on a moving target
- [/tg aim switch] three targets, swap between them
- Add a length: [/tg aim flick 60] (5 to 300 seconds, default 30)
- [/tg aim stop] ends it early

> Also in the menu: [/tg] > [Aim trainer].
> For the AI: [Train] > [Practice...] > pick [Aim trainer], start a session, then press [Start practicing].

=== matches | Arenas and matches
# 1. Save an arena
1. Build (or pick) a fighting area
2. Stand at one corner: [/tg arena pos1], at the opposite corner: [/tg arena pos2]
3. [/tg arena create duel1] saves it. It resets to this state every round
4. Optional: stand where players should start and type [/tg arena spawn duel1]

# 2. Make a match
- [/tg minigame duel duel1 m1] a ready-made match named m1
- Other templates: [sumo], [ffa], [team_deathmatch], [survival], [random]

# 3. Play it
1. [/tg match join m1 red]
2. [/tg match start m1]
3. Rounds repeat by themselves; [/tg match stop] ends it

# Choose the opponents
- [/tg match opponent m1 blue godly] (any dummy style)
- [... blue league] picks the style it loses to most, every round
- [... blue self] a past version of your AI fights it (self-play)

> Matches are where practice, exams and teacher demos work best: rounds reset automatically.

=== baritone | Baritone (what it does)
Baritone is NOT a fighter: it's a pathfinding bot (it walks, digs and mines by itself). It can't summon anything.

# What it's for here
- A teacher for movement: it plays as your AI while recording, so the AI learns to travel and mine
- Use it: put the optional Baritone jar in mods, then [Train] > [Teacher demos] > pick a [Baritone] teacher

# Looking for a fighting bot?
- That's a Training Grounds dummy: [/tg dummy spawn godly] (see "Fight a dummy")
- Or a teacher demo with the [Fighter] teacher, against a dummy

=== memory | Memory, allies and zones
# Places
- [F10] > [Memory...] shows a map and remembered places (base, chests, mines, danger)
- The AI marks places itself, or use the [Mark base] key

# Allies and enemies
- [Allies & enemies] lists players, teams and shield/banner "insignia" it has seen
- Mark someone friend or enemy; it treats people with the same insignia the same way

# Protected zones
- Stand in a friend's base, press [Protect area here]
- The AI will NEVER break or use anything inside it (size in Settings)

> Zones are a hard rule, not a preference: the AI's clicks there are simply blocked.

=== realism | Realism
Makes it play like a person, not a robot.

1. [Train] screen > [Realism...]
2. Press [Analyze]: learns how your play differs from the AI's
3. Press [Use my turning]: the AI turns and aims as fast and smoothly as you
4. Press [Which was better?]: watch two short clips, pick the better one. Do 10+

> After picking clips, press [Analyze] again. Practice then rewards your style.

=== video | Video Lab
Learn from gameplay videos (YouTube downloads, your own clips).

1. [F10] > [Video Lab...] > [Folder]: put videos in it
2. [Train input guesser] (once, and again after recording more)
3. [Build atlas] (once per resource pack or modpack)
4. Add tags (e.g. my-smp), press [Import]
5. Answer [Questions] about items it didn't recognise

# What happens
- It keeps first-person gameplay only and blanks out facecams/watermarks
- It guesses the keys and mouse, and saves each part as a recording
- Answers are saved per tag, so the next video with that tag doesn't ask again

! Video demos count half as much as your own recordings: guessed inputs are less sure.

=== players | Learning from players
Every other player you see while recording can become a lesson.

1. Record while others play near you (any server you're allowed to record on)
2. [Video Lab...] > [Learn from other players in recordings]
3. New recordings appear, one per player: tick them in [Train]

- It copies what led to hits and kills, and ignores what led to them getting hurt or dying
- Watching a Flashback replay while recording works too

# Exact mode (Training Grounds)
- [/tg record on] sends players' exact keys (everyone is told)
- Players can opt out with [/tg record optout]

! Only record people where that's allowed.

=== autonomy | Settings and safety
[F10] > [Settings...] > Autonomy and safety:
- [Respawn by itself] when it dies
- [Stop after] N hours, or after N deaths
- [Stop if health stays critical]
- [Canned chat replies] off by default; replies are tagged [AI]
- [Protected zone size]

# Built-in safety
- [F9] always stops it instantly
- If it gets stuck, it tries another way, then gives the goal up
- With no goals it idles naturally instead of freezing

=== help | Troubleshooting
# It does nothing / stands still
- Is the brain set up? ([Brain...] should say Running)
- Is it trained? Untrained AIs only follow simple goals
- Try [F8] again, or give it a goal

# Training is slow
- Use [Plan: Quick try] to test, [PC: Max] overnight
- Close other programs; Lite is much faster than VPT

# It got worse after training
- [Train] > [Versions...] > pick an older version > [Go back]

# Where are my files?
- .minecraft/minigamebots/ : profiles, recordings, videolab, logs
- Logs (for bug reports): minigamebots/brain/logs

> Still stuck? Send the log files: they say what went wrong.
